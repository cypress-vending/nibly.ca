What's the best kind of vending business to start?

For most people starting a vending business, the best kind is a connected smart vending machine. That is where vending is growing: customers want cashless payments, and operators want remote monitoring to save them work.1 Nibly, from Cypress Vending Corp in North Vancouver, BC, sells smart vending machines built this way.

Do vending machines still need to take cash, or is cashless enough now?

Cashless now covers most sales. In 2025, 78% of vending sales were cashless, up from 73% the year before, according to an industry payments report from July 2026.2

Contactless made up 85% of those cashless sales. Most vending customers now pay by tapping a card or phone.2

In metro areas, cashless acceptance is already above 80%. A September 2026 market report found that operators there see smarter machines as the next way to raise sales from each machine.3

The question now is what else a machine does with each sale. You can see what the Nibly machine does and judge for yourself.

How can I tell if a vending business opportunity is legit?

Do your own research and get independent advice before you decide. Canada's Competition Bureau gives that advice for any business opportunity.4

Check these with any seller:

  • The seller has a street address, a phone number and an email you can use
  • You can speak to a person before you pay anything
  • What comes with the machine is set out in writing
  • Support, service and warranty terms are written down
  • Any earnings figure you are shown has a source you can check
  • Someone with no stake in the sale has read the terms

Do all connected vending machines do the same thing?

No. About 35% of installed connected vending machines are still telemetry-only fleets, which give operators remote alerts and basic stock counts, according to a September 2026 market report.5 Ask what any connected machine does beyond reporting stock.
